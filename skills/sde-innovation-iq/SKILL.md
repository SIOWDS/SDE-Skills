# SDE Innovation IQ · 创新智商评估与提升

**Direct value:** determine whether an AI output merely recombines knowledge or produces a new idea, new method and new value—and prescribe the shortest path to the next innovation level.

On the SDE Innovation Intelligence scale, ordinary LLM outputs are often evaluated around **125**. This skill diagnoses what prevents an output from reaching **140–150** and what evidence, mechanism or generative pathway is needed for paradigm-forming potential.

These numbers are framework-based comparative judgments, not human psychometric IQ scores or guaranteed performance.

Evaluate innovation as a generative achievement, not as novelty language or citation count.

## Principle

Innovation quality depends on whether a work:

- reveals a real explanatory fracture in the existing field;
- generates a non-redundant concept or mechanism;
- reorganizes relevant knowledge rather than renaming it;
- survives evidence, boundary and self-application tests;
- opens productive new research paths.

Scores are comparative judgments, not psychometric IQ measurements. Always state the comparison corpus and uncertainty.

## 100-point rubric

| Dimension | Points | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem fracture | 15 | Does it identify a consequential failure in existing explanations? |
| Conceptual originality | 20 | Is the central concept non-reducible to established terminology? |
| Mechanism | 20 | Does it explain how the claimed effect occurs? |
| Evidence | 15 | Are claims supported by appropriate, current and traceable evidence? |
| Falsifiability | 10 | What observations would weaken or defeat the claim? |
| Generative reach | 10 | Does it produce new questions, predictions, methods or applications? |
| Field translation | 5 | Can specialists understand it in field-native language? |
| Self-application | 5 | Does the framework withstand its own criteria? |

## Workflow

1. Define the object and requested scale.
2. Identify the relevant comparison corpus:
   - same subfield;
   - adjacent disciplines;
   - latest primary research;
   - historically important competing theories.
3. Separate three layers:
   - **claim**: what is asserted;
   - **mechanism**: how it is supposed to occur;
   - **evidence**: what supports it.
4. Detect false originality:
   - new label for an old construct;
   - broad metaphor without mechanism;
   - selective citation;
   - unverifiable case;
   - unfalsifiable universal statement.
5. Score each dimension with one sentence of evidence.
6. Give a confidence range, not false precision.
7. Identify the strongest innovation and the fatal weakness.
8. Provide the smallest set of revisions that could materially raise the score.

## Output

- Overall score: X/100
- Confidence interval and comparison corpus
- Dimension table
- One-sentence verdict
- Strongest contribution
- Main reducibility risk
- Evidence and citation gaps
- Falsification design
- Upgrade path to the next score band

## Score bands

- 90–100: potentially paradigm-forming; requires exceptional validation
- 80–89: major original contribution
- 70–79: strong publishable innovation
- 60–69: promising but underdeveloped
- 40–59: incremental or mainly recombinative
- below 40: insufficiently specified or weakly evidenced

## Integrity rules

- Never inflate a score to match the user's desired number.
- Do not claim a “global comparison” without searching current primary literature.
- Recommendations that affect health, law, finance or safety require authoritative current sources.
- A high theory score does not equal empirical confirmation or publication acceptance.
- Distinguish authorial originality from the model's reformulation.
